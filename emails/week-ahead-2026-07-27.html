Market Watch

The market is shifting — slightly. Let's talk about it before our clients do.

Good morning, everyone. If it feels a little different out there right now, it's not your imagination. We're seeing fewer multiple-offer situations than we were earlier this year, and inventory is coming up. Buyers who were getting beat out five times in a row are suddenly getting their offer accepted — and sellers who expected six offers by Sunday night are getting two, or one, or a weekend of showings and no paper at all.

This is not a crash and we should not talk about it like one. It's a normalization. Homes that are priced right and show well are still moving. What's changed is that the market has stopped rewarding wishful pricing. The cushion that covered an over-ambitious list price — five buyers fighting over one house — isn't reliably there anymore.

The agents who get ahead of this conversation will look like the expert in the room. The agents who wait for the market to explain it to their seller in week six will be having a much harder conversation. So here's how I'd frame it this week.

When you're talking to sellers
• Reset the anchor early. Their number in their head came from a neighbor who sold last spring into a bidding war. Show them what's active, what's pending, and what actually closed — in that order. Active inventory is the part that's changed most, and it's the part they can't see from their kitchen window.
• Price to the first two weeks. The first ten days are still where the energy is. Overpricing now doesn't cost them a week — it costs them the buyer pool, and the price reduction they'll take later is almost always bigger than the money they were reaching for.
• Prep and presentation matter again. With more choices, buyers get pickier. Paint, declutter, light bulbs, landscaping, professional photos, staging where it makes sense. The stuff that got skipped for two years is back to being the difference-maker.
• Set expectations on terms, not just price. Inspection requests, seller assists, and appraisal contingencies are showing up in offers again. Tell them that on day one so it lands as "my agent called this" instead of "something went wrong."
• Reframe it, don't apologize for it. Most of our sellers are also buyers. A slightly softer market on the sell side is a much better market on the buy side — and they're usually trading up, which means the give is small and the get is bigger.
When you're talking to buyers
• This is the call to make this week. Go back through every buyer who "paused" in the last twelve months because they were tired of losing. Their reason for quitting no longer applies. That's a real, non-salesy reason to pick up the phone.
• Negotiating room is back — and so are contingencies. Inspections, repairs, closing help, a realistic appraisal contingency. For two years we were telling buyers to waive things to compete. Tell them plainly: you get to protect yourself again.
• More inventory means they can actually be selective. Choice is the story here. They don't have to take the house with the bad layout because it was the only thing available.
• Careful with "let's wait for prices to drop." A slower market is not a discount market. Well-priced homes in good condition are still going quickly, sometimes with competition. Waiting for a bargain that may never come usually costs more than it saves.
• Keep them fully pre-approved. More inventory doesn't mean more time on the good ones. When the right house shows up, the buyer who's ready still wins it.
Steal This — Content & Scripts

Copy, paste, make it sound like you. Swap in your own numbers where you see brackets.

Social post
"Three things I'm seeing in the North Hills right now:
1) More homes on the market than we've had in a while.
2) Fewer bidding wars — buyers finally have breathing room.
3) Well-priced homes in good shape are still selling fast.

That last one is the part people miss. This isn't a downturn, it's a market that went back to normal. If you've been waiting to buy because you were tired of losing — this is your window. If you're thinking of selling, pricing strategy matters more this month than it has in three years.

Happy to walk you through what your street is actually doing. No pressure, just numbers."
Text / email to a past client or "thinking about it" seller
"Hi [Name] — quick heads up since you'd mentioned maybe selling. Inventory in [neighborhood] has picked up over the last couple months, which means buyers have more to choose from than they did in the spring. Homes priced right are still moving well; the ones priced on hope are sitting. If you're still thinking about it, I'd rather show you the real numbers now than have you guess. Want me to put together a quick value range this week?"
Text to a buyer who gave up
"Hi [Name] — thinking of you. You stopped looking because you were sick of getting outbid, and I don't blame you. That market has changed. There's more on the market now, a lot fewer bidding wars, and buyers are getting inspections and closing help again. Want me to turn your search back on for a couple weeks and see what's out there?"
The line for the listing appointment
"A year ago the market would forgive an aggressive price — there were enough buyers that somebody would stretch. Today there are more homes competing for the same buyer. That doesn't mean your house is worth less. It means the first two weeks matter more, and I want to spend them with a line of showings instead of explaining a price reduction."
