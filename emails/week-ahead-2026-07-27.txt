MARKET WATCH
The market is shifting — slightly. Let's talk about it before our clients do.

Good morning, everyone. If it feels a little different out there right now,
it's not your imagination. We're seeing fewer multiple-offer situations than we
were earlier this year, and inventory is coming up. Buyers who were getting
beat out five times in a row are suddenly getting their offer accepted — and
sellers who expected six offers by Sunday night are getting two, or one, or a
weekend of showings and no paper at all.

This is not a crash and we should not talk about it like one. It's a
normalization. Homes that are priced right and show well are still moving. What
has changed is that the market has stopped rewarding wishful pricing. The
cushion that covered an over-ambitious list price — five buyers fighting over
one house — isn't reliably there anymore.

The agents who get ahead of this conversation will look like the expert in the
room. The agents who wait for the market to explain it to their seller in week
six will be having a much harder conversation. So here's how I'd frame it this
week.


WHEN YOU'RE TALKING TO SELLERS
------------------------------
* Reset the anchor early. Their number came from a neighbor who sold last
  spring into a bidding war. Show them active, pending, and closed — in that
  order. Active inventory is the part that's changed most, and it's the part
  they can't see from their kitchen window.

* Price to the first two weeks. The first ten days are still where the energy
  is. Overpricing now doesn't cost them a week — it costs them the buyer pool,
  and the reduction they'll take later is almost always bigger than the money
  they were reaching for.

* Prep and presentation matter again. With more choices, buyers get pickier.
  Paint, declutter, light bulbs, landscaping, professional photos, staging
  where it makes sense.

* Set expectations on terms, not just price. Inspection requests, seller
  assists, and appraisal contingencies are showing up in offers again. Tell
  them on day one so it lands as "my agent called this" instead of "something
  went wrong."

* Reframe it, don't apologize for it. Most sellers are also buyers. A slightly
  softer sell side is a much better buy side — and they're usually trading up,
  so the give is small and the get is bigger.


WHEN YOU'RE TALKING TO BUYERS
-----------------------------
* This is the call to make this week. Go back through every buyer who "paused"
  in the last twelve months because they were tired of losing. Their reason for
  quitting no longer applies.

* Negotiating room is back — and so are contingencies. Inspections, repairs,
  closing help, a realistic appraisal contingency. Tell them plainly: you get
  to protect yourself again.

* More inventory means they can actually be selective. They don't have to take
  the house with the bad layout because it was the only thing available.

* Careful with "let's wait for prices to drop." A slower market is not a
  discount market. Well-priced homes in good condition still go quickly,
  sometimes with competition.

* Keep them fully pre-approved. More inventory doesn't mean more time on the
  good ones.


STEAL THIS — CONTENT & SCRIPTS
------------------------------
[SOCIAL POST]
"Three things I'm seeing in the North Hills right now:
1) More homes on the market than we've had in a while.
2) Fewer bidding wars — buyers finally have breathing room.
3) Well-priced homes in good shape are STILL selling fast.

That last one is the part people miss. This isn't a downturn, it's a market
that went back to normal. If you've been waiting to buy because you were tired
of losing — this is your window. If you're thinking of selling, pricing
strategy matters more this month than it has in three years.

Happy to walk you through what your street is actually doing. No pressure,
just numbers."

[TEXT / EMAIL — PAST CLIENT OR "THINKING ABOUT IT" SELLER]
"Hi [Name] — quick heads up since you'd mentioned maybe selling. Inventory in
[neighborhood] has picked up over the last couple months, which means buyers
have more to choose from than they did in the spring. Homes priced right are
still moving well; the ones priced on hope are sitting. If you're still
thinking about it, I'd rather show you the real numbers now than have you
guess. Want me to put together a quick value range this week?"

[TEXT — BUYER WHO GAVE UP]
"Hi [Name] — thinking of you. You stopped looking because you were sick of
getting outbid, and I don't blame you. That market has changed. There's more on
the market now, a lot fewer bidding wars, and buyers are getting inspections
and closing help again. Want me to turn your search back on for a couple weeks
and see what's out there?"

[THE LINE FOR THE LISTING APPOINTMENT]
"A year ago the market would forgive an aggressive price — there were enough
buyers that somebody would stretch. Today there are more homes competing for
the same buyer. That doesn't mean your house is worth less. It means the first
two weeks matter more, and I want to spend them with a line of showings instead
of explaining a price reduction."
